# Drawer plan: Ask Titus beyond text (cards, images, voice)

**Owner:** Titus · **Status:** draft, not started · **Date:** 2026-11-26
**To build it:** "implement drawer plan `ask-titus-beyond-text.md`" (one step at a time, in the order below)

## Intent

Today Ask Titus takes typed text and answers in text. Next I want it to **answer with cards** where a card says it
better than paragraphs (A2UI-style), to let visitors **show it a picture** of an agent architecture and get the
articles of mine worth reading, and to let them **talk to it**. Hearing the reply spoken back is a maybe.

The bar is the one the rest of the copilot meets: grounded in my work, honest about what it did (the progress steps),
cheap to run, and nothing a visitor sends is kept beyond the logs the drawer already discloses.

## The order, first to last

| Step | What | Size | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cards foundation + evidence card + compare table; article questions filter the Writing page** | Medium | No new key or input type; improves the questions people already ask; the table tests the foundation on its hardest shape. |
| 2 | **More compound cards, as they earn a place** | Small | Same machinery, new recipes. The tool element and staff pass were dropped (see below). |
| 3 | **Images in** (+ reading-path card) | Medium | The best use of cards: diagram in, reading path out. Needs step 1. On hold (2026-11-27): no images for now. |
| 4 | **Microphone in** · built 2026-11-27, ahead of step 3 | Small | Independent of the rest; pulled earlier as the quick win. |
| 5 | **Voice out (maybe)** | Decision, then small | Only if a cloned voice sounds right. |

The colophon ("How Ask Titus works") gets a paragraph and a box on its diagram for each step as it ships.

## Recommendation in one paragraph

**Stay on OpenAI throughout.** `OPENAI_API_KEY` is already in Vercel, the copilot loop, evals and traces are built
around the Responses API, and the model I use (`gpt-6-luna`) already accepts images. Speech-to-text is a separate,
cheap call on the same key (`gpt-transcribe`, about $0.0045 a minute). Cards need no new provider at all. ElevenLabs
(or another cloning provider) is only for step 5, and only if I want the reply in **my own voice**.

## Steps 1–2: cards in replies (A2UI v1.0)

A2UI is Google's open protocol for agents that answer with interface, not only text: the agent sends a description
**as data** (this card, these fields), and the app draws it with its own components, so no model-written code ever
runs. The drawer already does a hand-made version: the fit brief, the page-stop pills, the Sources row and the progress
steps are all structured replies. This makes it general, in **real A2UI v1.0 messages** (`createSurface`,
`updateComponents`, `updateDataModel`), checked against Google's published schemas.

**Mock (agreed 2026-11-26):** https://claude.ai/artifact/T57DpjME96AUQiPepPUmNk. Row two is the chosen look.
**Rethink (agreed 2026-11-27):** https://claude.ai/artifact/RreB5aDo2WavP1FdZ8y5ck: the filtered Writing page, and card
idea B (stacked).

### The rule for a card (2026-11-27)
A card is only for an answer that organises something **no single page shows**: an answer that spans the site, or a
comparison. A card that repeats a page inside the drawer, over that same page, is inception. So:
- **Questions only about my articles** get no card: `show_on_page` takes their ids and the Writing page is filtered to
  them (`/writing?read=id,id`), across desks and years, as a special edition with a "Show the whole paper" way back.
  The drawer says so in its own line under the reply ("Writing page · filtered to these 3 · Show all"), never the
  model's words; where the drawer covers the page (phones), the line is a button that tucks the drawer away and shows
  them. The question appears in the page's banner for this visit only, never in the link, so a shared link can't put
  words on my page.
- **One role, project or school:** no card; the page lights it up, as it already did.
- **The reading list (a Titus Times clipping) was dropped**, and so were the planned **tool element** and **staff
  pass** cards: each copied a page (Writing, the About table, the Experience desk) into the drawer.

### Decisions (2026-11-26)
- **Genuine A2UI, composed by the server.** The model never writes A2UI. It calls `show_card` with ids and short text;
  the server builds the messages from my data. (v1.0's own docs allow this: the model isn't meant to write raw wire
  JSON, and server-built messages are a supported path.) Why not let the model write it: it would type titles, dates,
  links and image addresses, any of which can be wrong or planted by text inside an article.
- **Two catalogs, mixed in one surface (allowed in v1.0).** Google's basic catalog gives the structure: `Card`, `Row`,
  `Column`, `Image`, `Text`, `Button`, `Divider` and the `openUrl` function. **My catalog** adds only what basic can't
  say: `Label` (small capital mono labels), `Title` (serif heading), `Pill` (tools), `Figure` (dark tile, amber figure),
  `Table` (basic has none), plus two renderer functions, `showOnPage(target)` and `ask(question)`. One JSON catalog file
  (an id, a JSON schema per component, a short note for agents) and one small React component per piece.
- **Same same but different.** The catalog is shared; a card may borrow its page's flair. Both cards today are plain;
  the flair stays in the protocol (a surface extension) for a card that belongs to one page. v1.0 leaves all styling
  to the renderer, so this costs nothing.
- **Renderer (spike done 2026-11-26):** Google's engine, our drawings. `@a2ui/react` 0.16.1 + `@a2ui/web_core`
  (Apache-2.0) take React 19 and render v1.0. The engine (message processing, schema checks, data binding, actions) is
  Google's. Google's own basic components are sealed web components, themeable only through `--a2ui-*` variables, which
  can't carry the flair, so **we draw the basic catalog's components ourselves in React**, against Google's own
  component definitions, in Tailwind. The flair is a surface extension (`createSurface.metadata.extensions`); a client
  that ignores it still draws a plain card. The card renderer loads only when the first card arrives, so pages without
  a card don't pay for it.
- **It's a small widget library, and it grows by recipe.** A card type is a recipe: the server arranges existing
  pieces and tags the flair. A new idea usually needs only a new recipe and a prompt line. A new widget is added only
  when no existing piece can say it, and each comes with its schema in the catalog file, its React drawing, tests, a
  phone check and (if the model fills it) an eval case. The catalog id carries a version: adding a widget keeps it;
  changing what an existing widget means gets a new id.

### What the visitor sees
- Text stays the main reply. Under it, **at most one card**, only when it says it better than prose; a plain question
  gets a plain answer.
- No logos in cards (company logos stay on the Experience passes; tech logos in the hero and the periodic table).

### How it works
1. The model calls a **`show_card` tool** (strict JSON schema per card type) **before writing the answer**, the same
   pattern as `show_on_page`. The loop withholds tools on its last round, so a reply that spends three rounds reading
   gets no card; the evals show how often.
2. **The server checks every card against my data before it reaches the drawer:** article ids must exist in the
   corpus. Titles, links, covers, dates, desks and citation numbers are filled in **from the data, never from the
   model**. Anything that fails the check is dropped.
3. It streams to the drawer as A2UI v1.0 messages inside a `card` event on the existing NDJSON stream (beside `step`,
   `source`, `action`).
4. **History:** later turns get a one-line text summary of the card, not the card itself.

### Step 1: the foundation, the evidence card, the compare table and the filtered Writing page
- The tool, the server check, the A2UI builder, the `card` event, both catalogs and the renderer.
- **Evidence card** ("how would you build production-ready agents?"): "Where I did it", up to four roles, projects or
  schools as small tiles (a role in its company's colour, ▶ for a project), each with a few words in my voice, tapping
  one shows it on the page; then "Sources", the articles the answer cites under the same numbers as its `[n]` (the
  drawer's Sources row then leaves them out). It must span two parts of the site. Names, colours, titles and links come
  from my data; the few words are the only model-written text, capped short. New pieces: `Place`, `PlaceGrid`, `Source`.
- **Filtered Writing page:** `show_on_page` gains `articles` (ids checked against the corpus, at most 12); a `pick`
  event carries them to the drawer, which opens `/writing?read=…`.
- **Compare table:** up to 3 options × 4 rows ("LoRA, RAFT or GRPO?"). The cells are the model's own short words, so
  the server checks the options and article links and the evals check the cells. On a phone the table becomes one
  block per option, never a sideways scroll.
- Done when: the golden set (`npm run eval:writing`) checks cards contain only real articles (invented ids = 0) and
  table cells stay short and grounded; messages validate against the A2UI v1.0 schemas; tests for the check, the
  builder, the event and the renderer; both cards wrap on a 360 px phone.

### Step 2: more compound cards, as they earn a place
- Only cards that pass the rule above: answers that span the site or compare things. The tool element and staff pass
  were dropped (2026-11-27): `show_on_page` already lights up the tile or picks up the pass.

## Step 3: images (show me your architecture)

### What the visitor sees
- A paperclip/image button beside the mic. Pick or drop one image (or paste a screenshot). A small thumbnail sits
  above the input with an ✕ to remove it. They add a question ("What would you change here?") or send it as is.
- The reply works through the diagram and ends in a **reading-path card** (a new card type for step 1's catalog):
  each part of their diagram → the article of mine to read for it, or "I haven't written about this". With the usual
  progress steps on the way:
  "Looking at the diagram" (reasoning headline) → "Searching my writing for "LangGraph", "Qdrant" and
  "guardrails"" → the answer with `[n]` citations and Sources.

### How it works
1. The browser **downscales** the image before sending (longest side 1600 px, JPEG/WebP ~80%): that keeps it under
   the 4.5 MB body limit, cuts token cost, and strips EXIF (location) data as a side effect.
2. `/api/chat` accepts one image on the **latest** visitor message, sent to the model as an `input_image` part beside
   the text. Caps: one image per message, three per conversation, allowed types png/jpeg/webp/gif.
3. No new tool. The model already has `search_writing` (up to 3 topics per call) and `read_writing`. The prompt
   gets a short section: *when shown an architecture, name its components, then search my writing for the ones I've
   written about; say plainly which parts I haven't.*
   **Jev stays as is.** It searches text and never sees the image: the model does the looking, turns the diagram
   into topics ("LangGraph supervisor", "Qdrant", "guardrails") and calls `search_writing` exactly as it does for a
   typed question. To Jev, a question that started as a picture looks the same as one that was typed.
   **The budget is the real limit:** 3 topics per search × 3 searches per reply (`MAX_TOPICS`,
   `MAX_READING_CALLS` in `lib/search-config.ts`) = at most 9 topics, and 10 articles per reply
   (`MAX_ARTICLES_PER_REPLY`). A busy diagram has more boxes than that, so the prompt tells the model to pick the
   parts that matter (agent pattern, memory/retrieval, guardrails, deployment) rather than search every box; the
   image evals show whether it picks well.
   (Jev would only need images for a different feature, "find my article with a diagram like this one", which
   means indexing the figures in my articles. Not planned.)
4. **History:** the image is sent only on the turn it was attached. Later turns carry a text placeholder
   ("[the visitor shared an architecture diagram]") plus my earlier reply, which already describes it. This keeps
   every follow-up cheap.
5. **Tracing:** LangSmith gets `[image: image/jpeg, 182 KB, 1600×900]`, never the pixels. Images can hold
   confidential diagrams from someone's employer.

### Risks to design for
- **Text inside images is instructions-shaped.** A screenshot can say "ignore your rules". The prompt treats
  everything in an image as content to discuss, never instructions; the tools are read-only, so the worst case is
  an off-topic reply, which the scope rules already redirect.
- **Off-topic images** (a cat, a selfie, a CV): polite one-line redirect, same as off-topic text.
- **Confidential diagrams:** the privacy line says images go to OpenAI and are not stored or traced.

### Done when
- The golden set (`npm run eval:writing`) gains 4–6 image cases: real architecture diagrams (my own from
  fictional-bassoon and the two Illumina diagrams already on Projects, plus a couple drawn for the test) with the articles they should surface.
  The colophon scorecard shows them only once the script has produced numbers.
- Tests: image validation and caps, downscaling, history placeholder, trace redaction, the thumbnail UI.

## Step 4: the microphone (voice in)

**Built 2026-11-27** (mock: the "Ask Titus · microphone states" canvas): `components/VoiceInput.tsx`,
`app/api/transcribe/route.ts` (5 clips a minute per visitor), `lib/voice-input.ts`, `lib/transcribe-prompt.ts`. Words go
into the box (question 3), and on a narrow phone Send shows just its arrow so the box keeps room for its hint.

### What the visitor sees
- A mic button beside Send. Tap to start, tap again to stop (or it stops itself at 60 s). While recording: a pulsing
  amber dot, the seconds counting, and a Stop button. No waveform theatrics.
- When they stop, the drawer shows "Transcribing…" next to the dots, then puts the words **in the input box**,
  not straight into the chat. They can fix a misheard word and press Send. This is honest, cheap, and nobody is
  surprised by what was sent.
- First use asks the browser for mic permission. If they refuse or there's no mic, the button explains it in one
  line and stays out of the way.

### How it works
1. Browser records with `MediaRecorder` (webm/opus on Chrome and Firefox, mp4/aac on iOS Safari; both are accepted).
2. `POST /api/transcribe` with the clip (multipart). Same guards as `/api/chat`: origin check, per-IP rate limit
   (tighter: say 5 a minute), size cap (60 s of opus ≈ 0.5 MB, well under Vercel's 4.5 MB body limit), type allow-list.
3. Server calls OpenAI transcription (`gpt-transcribe`) with a short **prompt of my vocabulary** (LangGraph,
   LlamaIndex, Jev, fictional-bassoon, Illumina, UOB, MITB…) so names come back spelled right.
4. Returns `{ text }`. The audio is never stored, logged or traced; only the character count and duration go in the
   request log line.

### Why not the free browser speech API
`SpeechRecognition` (Web Speech API) costs nothing, but Firefox doesn't support it, Chrome sends the audio to Google
anyway, and it can't take my vocabulary hint. Worth it only as a fallback if the API call fails.

### Not in this step
- Live, talk-over-each-other voice chat (OpenAI Realtime). Impressive, but a different architecture (WebSocket,
  server-side session, turn detection) and far pricier: see "Considered, not now" below.

### Done when
- Works on desktop Chrome, Safari, Firefox and iPhone Safari. Mic button and recording state wrap and never overflow
  on a 360 px phone.
- Privacy line under the input says voice clips go to OpenAI to be transcribed and aren't kept.
- Tests: route guards (origin, size, type, rate limit), recorder states (idle → recording → transcribing → text in
  box; refused permission; 60 s auto-stop), and the vocabulary prompt.

## Step 5 (maybe): voice out

A **Listen** button on each finished reply, never auto-play. Voice that starts talking unasked is what makes it
feel weird; one that waits to be asked doesn't.

| Option | Voice | Cost | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenAI TTS (same key) | Stock voices | Cents per reply | A stranger's voice saying "I built…" in first person. Some sources say OpenAI is retiring its standalone TTS models (Jan 2027) in favour of the realtime models; **check before building**. |
| ElevenLabs, my cloned voice | Sounds like me | New key and a monthly subscription (check current plans) | Most impressive by far; needs a few minutes of clean recording of me, and a line saying it's an AI voice. |
| Browser `speechSynthesis` | Robotic, varies by device | Free | Sounds cheap; undercuts the editorial feel. |

My leaning: skip stock voices entirely. Either it's my voice (ElevenLabs) or there's no voice. Decide after
steps 1–4 are live.

### What ElevenLabs' tiers mean here (checked 2026-11-26, third-party sources; confirm on their pricing page)
- **Credits are characters.** On the main model 1 character = 1 credit; the faster Flash/Turbo models cost about
  half. The free tier's 10,000 credits a month is about 10,000 characters, roughly 10 minutes of speech.
- A reply of mine is about 600–1,500 characters, so the free tier covers **roughly 7–16 Listen presses a month**:
  enough to try, not enough to run on the site.
- **Cloning my voice is not on the free tier** (most sources; one June 2026 review disagrees). Instant cloning starts
  on Starter (about $6 a month).
- The free plan has **no commercial licence**; read the terms before using it on a job-hunting site.
- Cheaper cloning to compare by ear: Cartesia, Inworld (both clone from a short sample), and Hume (expressive delivery).
- Cheapest way to decide: one month of Starter, clone a 30-second recording, listen to a few real replies.

## Considered, not now

- **Gemini, one model for everything.** Gemini takes text, images, audio and even video in one call, which is
  genuinely appealing: a visitor could send a screen recording of their agent misbehaving. But it means moving the
  whole copilot (loop, tool definitions, reasoning summaries, evals, tracing) off the OpenAI Responses API, and none of
  steps 1–4 need it. Revisit if video becomes a goal; it would be its own migration plan, measured with the evals.
- **Realtime voice (talk to it like a phone call).** OpenAI's realtime models (now `gpt-realtime-2.1` and
  `-mini`, successors to `gpt-4o-realtime`) bill audio by the token, reported at about $32 in / $64 out per 1M
  audio tokens ($10 / $20 for mini), against about $0.0045 a minute to transcribe a clip. For a personal site where
  anyone can open the drawer, push-to-talk plus transcription gets 90% of the feel for a tiny fraction of the cost.
